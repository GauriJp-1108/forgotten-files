EVIDENCE
DISPLAY CABINET EXAMINATION

The Display Cabinet

Examination of the cabinet that contained the Ashford Diamond Necklace.

Condition

The glass cabinet is intact. There are no cracks, scratches or signs that the lock was forced.

Lock

The locking mechanism appears normal from the outside. However, examination of the latch reveals that it can appear secured even when the locking mechanism has not fully engaged.

Significance

Whoever removed the necklace may not have needed to break into the cabinet at all. Someone familiar with the mechanism could open it without leaving obvious damage.

Investigator's Note

A locked cabinet does not necessarily mean a properly secured cabinet. Look carefully at what the physical evidence does — and does not — show.

EVIDENCE
SECURITY SYSTEM REPORT

Security Camera

Hallway security recording covering the study entrance.

Recording

The hallway camera was operating normally before the incident. At approximately 8:25 PM, the signal disappeared.

Duration

The camera remained offline until approximately 8:40 PM, creating a fifteen-minute blind spot during the investigation window.

Question

Was the camera failure simply a technical problem, or did someone deliberately create an opportunity to move around unseen?

EVIDENCE
FOOTWEAR EXAMINATION

Muddy Footprint

A partial shoe impression discovered near the study window.

Print

A muddy footprint was found near the study window. The impression is consistent with a formal leather shoe approximately US size 10.

Marcus

Marcus Reed wears approximately size 10 shoes and admitted that he went outside around 8:30 PM while the ground was wet.

Problem

The window sill itself contains no mud, and the flowerbed outside is undisturbed. The footprint may therefore not prove that anyone entered through the window.

Investigator's Note

The footprint makes the window theory tempting. But evidence should explain the entire scene, not just one suspicious detail.

EVIDENCE
STUDY WINDOW EXAMINATION

The Study Window

Examination of the supposed point of entry.

Condition

The study window was found open after the necklace disappeared.

Exterior

There are no obvious scratches, fingerprints or damaged surfaces around the window.

Ground

The flowerbed beneath the window remains undisturbed. There is no clear trail showing that someone climbed through the window.

Investigator's Note

An open window can tell a story — but it can also be part of a story someone wants investigators to believe.

EVIDENCE
TELEPHONE RECORDS

Daniel's Phone Records

Call history examined against Daniel's claimed whereabouts.

His Claim

Daniel said he went upstairs around 8:15 PM because he had an important phone call and remained upstairs until approximately 8:40 PM.

Records

The phone records show an actual call beginning at approximately 8:37 PM and ending at 8:44 PM.

Gap

The recorded call does not cover the period from approximately 8:20 PM to 8:37 PM — the first seventeen minutes of the disappearance window.

Investigator's Note

The phone call is real. The question is whether it proves where Daniel was before the call began.

EVIDENCE
DINING ROOM EXAMINATION

The Dining Table

Examination of the table and glasses after the necklace disappeared.

Glasses

Four wine glasses were found on the dining table, several of them still partially full.

Timeline

The glasses suggest that the guests were not continuously seated at the table throughout the critical period.

Significance

Statements claiming that everyone remained together at dinner should therefore be treated carefully.

EVIDENCE
KITCHEN EXAMINATION

The Kitchen

Examination of Thomas Blake's claimed location.

Dessert

Dessert preparation was underway in the kitchen, with an unfinished cake and an oven still running.

Access

The kitchen has a back entrance that could provide another route through the house.

Timing

The dessert did not require constant supervision, meaning Thomas's statement that he could not have left the kitchen cannot be confirmed by the food alone.

EVIDENCE
KEY EXAMINATION

The Spare Key

Eleanor's spare key to the study cabinet.

Location

The spare key was found inside the drawer in Eleanor's bedroom where she normally kept it.

Condition

The key appears to have remained in the drawer throughout the evening.

Significance

The thief apparently did not need to steal the spare key in order to access the cabinet.

EVIDENCE
SECURITY SYSTEM ANALYSIS

Camera System Knowledge

What the camera failure tells us about the person responsible.

Blind Spot

The hallway camera stopped recording precisely during the period in which the necklace disappeared.

Familiarity

Someone deliberately interfering with the system would need to know where the relevant equipment was and how to interrupt it.

Investigation

This makes knowledge of the manor and its systems potentially more important than simply being near the study.

Investigator's Note

Ask yourself: who knew the house well enough to move through it without attracting attention?
