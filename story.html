ASHFORD MANOR CASE #001

The Ashford Diamond Necklace

A quiet dinner. Five suspects. One missing necklace.

There was nothing unusual about the evening at first.

Ashford Manor had hosted dinners like this for years. The house was old, spacious and familiar to everyone who lived there. On Saturday evening, Eleanor Ashford welcomed four guests through the front doors.

It was 7:30 PM. Dinner had been planned for eight. Wine had already been poured, the kitchen was busy preparing the meal, and conversation filled the downstairs rooms.

✦

The Necklace

Among the Ashford family's most valuable possessions was the Ashford Diamond Necklace.

It had been in the family for nearly one hundred years. The necklace contained 23 diamonds, including one large blue diamond set in platinum.

Its estimated value was ₹2.4 crore.

Eleanor normally kept the necklace inside a locked glass display cabinet in the study. It was not something that could simply be forgotten on a table. The necklace had a specific place. A locked place.

7:30 PM Eleanor Ashford welcomes her guests to Ashford Manor.

8:20 PM

Approximately fifty minutes later, Eleanor decided to show the necklace to her guests.

She led everyone toward the study. The glass cabinet stood exactly where it always had.

Eleanor opened it herself.

For a few moments, everyone had a clear view of the necklace. The diamonds caught the light from the room. The large blue stone was impossible to miss.

Then Eleanor returned the necklace to the cabinet. She says she locked it.

She then returned to the dinner area.

✦

The Missing Fifteen Minutes

Five minutes later, something changed.

At approximately 8:25 PM, the hallway security camera stopped recording.

There was no warning. No obvious disturbance. The camera simply went offline.

For the next fifteen minutes, the hallway was outside the camera's view.

At exactly 8:40 PM, the system began recording again.

8:25 PM — 8:40 PM The hallway camera is offline.

8:43 PM

Three minutes after the camera returned, Eleanor went back to the study.

At first, nothing appeared unusual.

The glass cabinet was still standing. The room had not been destroyed. There was no broken glass. There were no obvious signs of forced entry.

Then Eleanor looked inside.

The necklace was gone.

✦

The House Is Sealed

Eleanor immediately called everyone into the study.

Nobody was allowed to leave Ashford Manor.

The police were contacted. They arrived approximately twenty-five minutes later.

What initially looked like a simple theft quickly became more complicated.

There were only five people who could realistically have taken the necklace.

Five people.

Five different stories.

01 Eleanor Ashford
02 Daniel Ashford
03 Marcus Reed
04 Clara Wilson
05 Thomas Blake

Something Doesn't Add Up

Every person in the house had a reason to attract suspicion.

Eleanor owned the necklace.

Daniel knew the house extremely well.

Marcus admitted that he had gone outside.

Clara knew the family's routines.

Thomas claimed he had never left the kitchen.

At first glance, almost every explanation seemed possible.

But a good investigation isn't about finding the person who looks the most suspicious.

It is about finding the person whose story explains everything.

INVESTIGATOR'S NOTE

Do not decide who is guilty yet. The statements are only the beginning. The crime scene may tell you something the suspects don't.
